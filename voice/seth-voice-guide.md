# Seth Caslin - Voice & Script Guide

*Reverse-engineered from 14 Instagram reels (@sethycaslin): spoken transcriptions + captions. Use this as the instruction set when writing new scripts in Seth's voice.*

*Built July 14, 2026*

How to use this: Sections 1-6 describe how Seth actually sounds, with real lines pulled from his videos. Section 7 is the guardrail (what to never write). Section 8 is a pass/fail checklist - run any draft script through it before calling it "Seth."

## 1. Tone & Personality

Seth is a 24-year-old real-estate flipper who plays a character: the unapologetic, money-obsessed "multi-millionaire who flips crack houses." The voice is built on a few load-bearing traits:

- **Cocky but self-aware.** He brags about the deal, then undercuts himself with a joke so it lands as fun instead of douchey.
- **Heavy, casual profanity.** Casual profanity is the texture of every video - "fucking" as an intensifier, "shitbox," "piece of shit." It's constant but natural, never bleeped-out edgelord for its own sake.
- **Dark humor about distressed property & sellers.** He jokes about meth heads, crack houses, evictions, gambling addicts and distressed sellers with zero hand-wringing. The darkness is played for comedy, not shock.
- **Politically provocative on purpose.** He deliberately pokes "raging liberals," "libtards," and anti-capitalists, then chalks up "another W in the win column for capitalism." It's bait, and he knows it.
- **Brainrot / internet-native.** Terminally-online phrasing - "mogs the shopping carts," "mom's basement maxing," "absolutely autistic amount of tabs." He talks like the comment section, not like a brand.
- **Receipts-forward.** Every claim is backed with "if you don't believe me, here's the house / the contract right here." Cocky, but always shows the receipts.

*Net feel: a friend who's clearly making real money, is a little bit of a menace about it, and is letting you in on the game while trolling everyone who's mad about it.*

## 2. Hook Patterns

The first sentence always does the work - a dollar figure, an outrageous claim, or both, BEFORE any intro. He never opens with "Hey guys, welcome back." There are four repeating formulas:

### Formula A - "Here's how I made / I'm gonna make $[figure]"

The cleanest workhorse hook. States the outcome and the number up front, framed as a reveal.

> "Here's how I made $75,000 flipping this piece of shit condo behind me."
>
> "Here's how I'm gonna make a teacher's salary just flipping this one crack house this summer."
>
> "Here's how my brand new $5 an hour virtual assistant from Egypt is about to make me $80,000 on one deal."
>
> "Easiest way to make 100K this summer. Send me pieces of shit like this."

### Formula B - Shock event / outrageous claim + profit anyway

Lead with something jarring (a fire, a crime, a wild character), then pivot to "but I still walked away with $[figure]." The disaster is the bait; the profit is the payoff.

> "A meth head torched the living fuck out of my kitchen at this property behind me, but I still walked away with $60,000 in profit."
>
> "My attorney thought I was absolutely crazy when I bought this deal."
>
> "I convinced the seller I was a liberal so she'd sell me this property, and now I'm gonna profit a quarter million dollars."

### Formula C - Outrageous line + fake-out ("just fucking around")

Say something deliberately over-the-line, let it hang, then yank it back. This is his signature comedic move - it lets him be shocking AND likable in the same breath.

> "The only thing separating me and $80,000 of profit is evicting a single mother of three from this shit box you see behind us. Just fucking with you, not this time at least."
>
> "A gambling addict inherited this shitbox behind me... he threw about half of it on the Spurs to close out game four at halftime. Nah, I'm just fucking around."

### Formula D - Third-party proof or location flex

Either put someone else's win up front ("Jack made $70,000...") or open on a flashy location as a status flex before the pitch. Both borrow credibility in the first three seconds.

> "Jack made $70,000 yesterday just by sending me this property."
>
> "All right, we're down at the Ritz over in Key Biscayne, Florida."
>
> "All right, we're here in Fairfield Beach, Connecticut. We're turning a two and a half million dollar house into our new sales office."

*Rule of thumb: if the first sentence doesn't contain a number, a crime, a character, or a flex, it's not a Seth hook.*

## 3. Script Structure

A typical deal video moves through the same five beats. Not every video hits all five, but the spine is consistent:

1. **Hook.** The hook (see Section 2). A number and/or an outrageous claim in sentence one, referencing the property "behind me / behind us."
2. **Self-intro.** Optional but frequent: "If you're new here, my name is Seth. I'm a 23-year-old multi-millionaire who flips crack houses just like this one." Dropped in AFTER the hook, never before it.
3. **Property backstory.** Who owned it and what was wrong - the meth head, the divorcing couple, the gambling addict, the title issues, the fire. This is where the dark comedy lives.
4. **Deal mechanics.** The plain-spoken math: "picked it up for about X, put about Y into it, on the market for Z." Walks through purchase -> rehab -> resale.
5. **Profit figure.** The number he's netting: "looking at about $80,000 of profit here." Always stated, always specific.
6. **Sign-off / CTA.** A mic-drop line or a CTA - "another W in the win column for capitalism," "off to the races," "hit my DMs," "follow for part two," or "time for a drink at this horribly staged table."

Example of the full arc in one video (the $75K condo):

> "Here's how I made $75,000 flipping this piece of shit condo behind me. If you're new here, my name is Seth. I'm a 23 year old who flips crack houses just like this one. Picked this condo up for about 175,000. Think we put about 45,000 into it. It's on the market, sold for 320,000... should be looking at about 75, 80 grand in profit... All you have to do is find a buyer like myself. I'll pay you 20, 30, even $50,000 to find me deals like this."

*Note the rhythm: short declarative sentences, present tense, "All right" as a scene-transition marker ("All right, we bought this guy a few weeks ago..."). He narrates like he's walking you through the house in real time.*

## 4. Signature Phrases & Vocabulary

### How he refers to properties

Never neutral. A property is almost always a slur-for-a-house:

- shitbox / shit box
- crack house
- piece of shit condo / pieces of shit
- dilapidated and unlivable property
- Always anchored in space: "behind me," "behind us," "you see behind me," "this one right here."

### How he presents himself

- "My name is Seth. I'm a 23-year-old multi-millionaire who flips crack houses."
- "If you're new here..." as the intro trigger.
- "I became a multi-millionaire this year at 23..." - age + net worth stated as fact, no false modesty.

### How he talks about profit & money

- "looking at about $X of profit here" / "should be looking at..."
- "bank $80,000," "net profit," "take home," "turn a profit," "grand," "K"
- "scooped this bad boy up for $450,000" / "picked it up for about..."
- Offers the viewer money: "I'll pay you 10, 20, even $50,000 to send me deals like this."
- Comparisons that sting: "make a teacher's salary just flipping this one crack house."

### Recurring expressions & tags

- "All right," - near-universal opener / scene transition
- "got her locked up" / "locked me up an absolute heater" (locked up = closed the deal)
- "off to the races" / "we're off to the races"
- "that's a fucking fire deal"
- "another W in the win column for capitalism"
- "if you don't believe me, here's the [house/contract] right here"
- "hit my DMs" / "rope me in" / "send me your deals"
- "the boys" / "the fellas" / "move some of the boys in"
- "Just fucking with you" / "Nah, I'm just fucking around" (the fake-out)

### Political & internet slang (used sparingly, for spice)

- "raging liberals," "libtards," "fucktard," "retard math," "the evil dictator," "anti-capitalism Reddit"
- "mogs the shopping carts," "mom's basement maxing," "absolutely autistic amount of tabs," "I love to troll"

## 5. Numbers Style

Numbers are the backbone of every video. He ALWAYS walks the full deal math - purchase price, rehab spend, ARV / sale price, and profit - even in a 25-second clip. The figures are what make the flex credible.

- Full chain, stated in order: buy price -> "put about X into it" -> resale / ARV -> net profit.
- Numbers are round and softened with "about" or "probably about" - he's estimating out loud, not reading a spreadsheet.
- He mixes formats naturally: "175,000," "45 grand," "80K," "a quarter million," "half a million," "$1mm."
- The profit line is almost always the last number and gets its own sentence: "about $80,000 of profit here."
- Fees are hand-waved as texture: "after agent fees, costs, everything," "all the other bullshit fees you have to pay."

**Real examples of the full math:**

> "We scooped this bad boy up for $450,000, and it's worth about $700,000 as is."
>
> "Picked this condo up for about 175,000. Think we put about 45,000 into it. It's on the market, sold for 320,000. So after agent fees, costs, everything, should be looking at about 75, 80 grand in profit."
>
> "If we're buying it for $65,000, we're going to put in about $35,000, and it should be worth about $200,000 when we're done. So between points, expenses, and all the other bullshit fees... about $80,000 of profit here."
>
> "Bought it for a total of $450,000 and put probably about $60,000 into it and now it's on the market for almost $700,000. We're looking at probably about $150,000 of profit here."

## 6. Caption Style

Captions follow a two-part pattern. Part one always fires; part two is optional.

### Part 1 - the punchy line (always present)

A short, provocative CTA or challenge, usually with one emoji. Types he uses:

- **"Tag a friend who..."** - "Tag a friend who would say anything to get the deal done 🤝" / "Tag a friend who always fucks up the deal by overcomplicating it"
- **"Send this to a friend who..."** - "Send this to a friend who's a gambling addict"
- **Direct challenge / question** - "What would you do?" / "@Realtors I challenge you to be this honest in your next house video! 😂"
- **Offer** - "SUMMER SIDE HUSTLE IDEA: send me shitboxes like this and i will pay you $20k-50k per deal..." / "Make 100k with me this summer!"
- **Pure provocation** - "Libtards HATE flipping houses... Go back to economics class, fucktard"
- **Series tease** - "OUR FIRST SERIES: Cold calling my haters moms... 😈 ... Make sure to follow for Part 2!"

### Part 2 - the explainer block (optional)

When present, it's separated from the hook by dash lines (`- - - -`) and does one of two jobs:

- The recurring bio boilerplate - "My name's Seth. I'm 24 years old, and I've built my business around finding opportunities where most people only see problems. Over the past few years, I've bought, renovated, and sold distressed properties, raised millions of dollars in private capital..."
- Or a plain-English recap of the specific deal's mechanics and why it was tricky.
- Occasional hashtags, kept minimal: #wholesalinghouses #wholesalingrealestate #wanghaf

*Important contrast: the Part-2 bio block is the ONE place Seth sounds polished and professional. The spoken video and the Part-1 caption line are raw and profane; the bio paragraph is clean corporate-lite. Keep that split - don't let the bio's tone bleed into the hook.*

## 7. What Seth Would NOT Say

Guardrails. If a draft contains any of the following, it's off-voice - cut it:

- **No corporate speak.** "We leverage our core competencies," "add value," "synergy," "passionate about real estate," "on a mission to help families." He never sounds like a brand deck.
- **No generic motivational quotes.** "Success is a journey," "rise and grind," "believe in yourself," vision-board platitudes. He shows the money instead of inspiring you toward it.
- **No sanitized real-estate euphemisms.** "charming fixer-upper," "cozy starter home," "hidden gem," "a property with great bones." It's a shitbox or a crack house, full stop.
- **No hedged / lawyered language.** "We hope to potentially generate a modest return," "results may vary." The only hedging allowed is "about" on a dollar figure. He never softens the claim itself.
- **No polished humility.** "So blessed," "grateful for this community," "couldn't have done it without you guys." No influencer gratitude theater.
- **No neutral / careful political tone.** He deliberately antagonizes; he does not play it safe or apologize for the tactics (he brags about "questionable sales tactics" with no disclaimer).
- **No over-polish.** No long, comma-spliced, sub-claused sentences in the spoken VO. Beyond a couple of hashtags, no hashtag walls. No emojis inside the spoken script (emojis live in captions only).

## 8. "Sounds Like Seth" Validation Checklist

Run any generated script through this before shipping it. Aim to hit essentially all of these; if it misses more than one or two, it's not Seth yet.

- [ ] **Hook lands in line one.** First sentence contains a specific dollar figure and/or an outrageous claim - before any intro.
- [ ] **Real numbers, full deal math.** At least one concrete dollar amount; ideally the full chain: buy price -> rehab -> resale/ARV -> profit.
- [ ] **Profit called out.** Profit is stated plainly in its own line ("about $X of profit here"), not buried or implied.
- [ ] **Property is trash-talked.** Property is called a shitbox / crack house / piece of shit - never a neutral "home" or "property."
- [ ] **Profanity feels native.** Casual "fucking"-style profanity used naturally as texture, not forced in for shock.
- [ ] **Grounded in the space.** A spatial reference - "behind me," "you see behind us," "right here."
- [ ] **Edgy backstory.** A character or backstory with an edge (meth head, gambling addict, divorcing couple, hater's mom, title nightmare).
- [ ] **Self-aware wink present.** Cocky but self-aware - at least one wink or fake-out ("just fucking with you"), so it reads fun not arrogant.
- [ ] **Right rhythm.** Short, present-tense, spoken-word sentences; "All right" as a transition; contractions throughout.
- [ ] **Strong sign-off / CTA.** Ends on a mic-drop line or CTA ("another W for capitalism," "off to the races," "hit my DMs," "follow for part two").
- [ ] **Viewer payout (if applicable).** Money offer to the viewer when relevant ("I'll pay you 10, 20, even $50k to send me deals").
- [ ] **No banned language.** Zero corporate speak, zero motivational fluff, zero sanitized euphemisms (see Section 7).
- [ ] **Caption follows two-part pattern.** Caption = punchy CTA / challenge line (+1 emoji), optionally followed by a dash-separated bio or deal-recap block.
